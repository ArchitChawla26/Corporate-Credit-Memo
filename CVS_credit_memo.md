# Corporate Credit Memo — CVS Health Corporation (NYSE: CVS)

**To:** Credit Committee
**From:** Archit Chawla
**Re:** CVS Health Corporation — senior unsecured credit review; FY2021–FY2025 and twelve months ended June 30, 2026
**Companion model:** `CVS_credit_model.xlsx` (live formulas; inputs in `data/inputs.json`)

*Independent research project prepared by Archit Chawla. This memo is not investment advice and is not a credit rating. All financial data are from CVS Health's SEC filings via EDGAR (CIK 0000064803).*

## Recommendation

**APPROVE — maintain investment-grade senior unsecured exposure to CVS Health at standard investment-grade terms, subject to the conditions in Section 8.** Shadow rating: **BBB, stable outlook.**

The credit has passed its stress point. Adjusted leverage peaked at 4.6x in FY2024, when the Aetna insurance book absorbed a Medicare Advantage cost surge and debt peaked at $66.3B. Since then the company has cut total debt by $4.9B to $61.4B, LTM adjusted EBITDA has recovered to $18.9B, FFO/debt is back to 24.7%, and free cash flow after dividends covered 13.6% of debt in the LTM period. Liquidity is strong and maturities are well laddered. The offsetting facts — a one-notch cushion above high yield at Moody's (Baa3), an earnings base that keeps absorbing litigation charges, and a $5.7B goodwill impairment that confirms overpayment for care delivery — keep this a BBB, not a BBB+.

## 2. Business profile & competitive position

I view CVS's business profile as the strongest part of the credit. It is one of the largest healthcare companies in the world by revenue ($402.1B in FY2025, +7.8% year over year), with three mutually reinforcing segments (FY2025 segment revenues, before $71.6B of intersegment eliminations):

- **Health Care Benefits (Aetna)** — $143.4B. Health insurance products serving more than 37 million people; premiums were $134.8B of consolidated FY2025 revenue.
- **Health Services** — $190.4B. Caremark PBM (approximately 87 million plan members), specialty pharmacy, and the Oak Street / Signify care-delivery assets.
- **Pharmacy & Consumer Wellness** — $139.4B. Approximately 9,000 retail locations and more than 1,000 walk-in and primary-care clinics; filled 1.8 billion prescriptions (30-day equivalents) in 2025.

Defensive characteristics: demand is non-discretionary and demographically supported; revenue is diversified across insurance premiums, PBM services and pharmacy dispensing; and the vertical model (insurer + PBM + pharmacy + clinics) gives CVS cost and data advantages competitors cannot easily replicate. The structural weaknesses are equally clear: consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin is only ~4–5%, so small swings in medical costs or drug mix move earnings materially; the PBM model faces persistent political and regulatory pressure on rebates and pricing; and insurance earnings are inherently estimate-driven (reserves, premium deficiency assessments).

## 3. Capital structure

All funded debt is senior unsecured at the parent, plus $1,326M of finance lease liabilities that are presented within the debt lines of the balance sheet (FY2025 10-K, leases note). There is no meaningful secured layer, so unsecured creditors are not structurally subordinated to secured claims at the operating level; the practical subordination is to insurance-subsidiary policyholder obligations, which are ring-fenced by state regulation.

The debt trajectory tells the acquisition story: CVS levered up for Aetna (2018, ~$69B), paid debt down through 2022, re-levered in 2023 to fund Signify Health and Oak Street Health, and peaked at $66.3B at year-end 2024. Deleveraging since then is real, not cosmetic: $3,287M of long-term debt was repaid in H1 2026 alone, commercial paper was taken to zero, and management partly term-funded the stack with $3.0B of junior subordinated hybrid notes issued in December 2024 ($2.25B 7.0% due March 2055; $750M 6.75% due December 2054), instruments structured to receive partial equity credit from the rating agencies. The dividend was held roughly flat ($3,397M paid in FY2025), so deleveraging has been funded from operating cash flow rather than shareholder-return cuts. Revolver covenants are customary and — importantly — contain no acceleration of maturities on a ratings downgrade (FY2025 10-K).

## 4. Ratio analysis

EBITDA is shown two ways. *Reported EBITDA* is GAAP operating income plus depreciation & amortization. *Adjusted EBITDA* adds back only items that are separate line items in the consolidated statements of operations or discrete charges quantified in the filings' segment reconciliations (goodwill impairment; opioid and legacy litigation charges; loss on assets held for sale; restructuring). I deliberately do **not** add back insurance premium deficiency reserves or medical cost pressure — those are real underwriting economics. FFO is an approximation, stated as such: net income + depreciation & amortization + goodwill impairment.

Three observations drive my assessment:

1. **FY2024 was the trough, and it was an underwriting event, not a demand event.** Health care costs rose from $86.2B (FY2023) to $115.1B (FY2024) as Medicare Advantage utilization spiked; Aetna recorded premium deficiency reserves, and segment adjusted operating income in Health Care Benefits collapsed to $307M. Adjusted EBITDA fell to $14.4B against peak debt — 4.60x gross, 5.86x lease-adjusted, FFO/debt of 13.9%. Those are weak-BBB metrics, and Moody's move to Baa3 reflects exactly this period.
2. **FY2025's reported numbers look worse than the credit.** GAAP operating income of $4,660M carries the $5,725M Q3 2025 goodwill impairment on the Health Care Delivery reporting unit (non-cash), plus $1,220M of legacy litigation charges and a $320M opioid accrual increase. On the adjusted basis leverage was 3.91x; on reported EBITDA it screens at 6.97x. A committee should hold both numbers in view: the impairment is non-cash, but it is also an admission that acquisition value was destroyed.
3. **The LTM period shows genuine repair.** Health Care Benefits adjusted operating income recovered to $2,939M in FY2025 (company segment measure), H1 2026 operating income rose to $9,383M from $5,755M, operating cash flow LTM is $14,780M, and FCF after dividends of $8,342M funded the paydown to $61.4B of debt. LTM FFO/debt of 24.7% and coverage of 6.08x are solidly mid-BBB metrics.

## 5. Liquidity & maturity profile

Liquidity is a strength. At June 30, 2026 CVS held $11,329M of cash (about $2,700M at the parent or non-restricted subsidiaries; the balance sits largely in regulated insurance entities) plus three undrawn $2.5B back-up revolving credit facilities ($7.5B total, expiring May 2029, 2030 and 2031 per the Q2 2026 10-Q) that backstop the commercial paper program, under which nothing was outstanding. Cash plus revolver capacity of ~$18.8B covers the remaining debt maturities many times over.

Maturity ladder (principal, at December 31, 2025, FY2025 10-K borrowings note):

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

68% of principal falls due after 2030 — a long, well-spread ladder with no single-year wall above ~$5.0B (≈8% of principal). The current portion of long-term debt had already fallen to $1,958M by June 30, 2026 after H1 repayments. Scheduled interest on the long-term debt is about $2,933M for 2026 (contractual obligations table). The main scheduled non-debt outflow is the opioid settlement: $3,975M of obligations at December 31, 2025, of which $648M falls within one year — pre-funded by accruals and easily absorbed by LTM operating cash flow of $14,780M.

## 6. Shadow rating assessment

**Shadow rating: BBB, stable outlook** (senior unsecured).

- **Business profile (supports BBB+/A−):** dominant, diversified, non-discretionary healthcare platform; $415B LTM revenue; leading positions in PBM, retail pharmacy and a top-tier health insurer.
- **Financial profile (BBB):** adjusted gross leverage of 3.26x LTM (3.91x FY2025) and lease-adjusted leverage of 4.12x sit in the BBB band; FFO/debt of 24.7% LTM and adjusted coverage of 6.08x are mid-BBB; FY2024 showed how fast these metrics can deteriorate (4.60x / 13.9%) when insurance underwriting turns.
- **Governance & policy (neutral to negative):** a decade of debt-funded acquisitions, recurring "one-off" litigation charges (FY2022 opioid $5,803M; FY2025 legacy $1,220M), and the Health Care Delivery impairment argue against a higher assessment despite demonstrated deleveraging since 2024.
- My BBB sits in line with S&P (BBB) and Fitch (BBB) and one notch above Moody's Baa3, which I read as pricing the lease-adjusted and trough-period metrics more heavily. **Upgrade path:** adjusted leverage sustained below 3.0x with FFO/debt above 30%. **Downgrade triggers:** adjusted leverage above 4.0x for two consecutive half-years, FFO/debt below 15%, a debt-funded acquisition above $5B, or an adverse litigation judgment materially above accruals.

## 7. Key risks & mitigants

- **Insurance underwriting volatility.** Medicare Advantage cost trends drove the FY2024 trough; FY2025 still absorbed premium deficiency reserves ($448M in Q1 2025 on the individual exchange book, with further reserves in Q2). *Mitigant:* repricing and benefit redesign flowed through — Health Care Benefits adjusted operating income recovered from $307M (FY2024) to $2,939M (FY2025).
- **Litigation overhang.** Beyond the accrued opioid obligations ($3,975M), CVS carries False Claims Act exposure from the April 2025 Omnicare jury verdict (the $1,220M legacy charges relate to two court decisions on past practices) and, per Dow Jones / Benzinga reports on October 14, 2026, agreed to pay $315M to resolve federal allegations over opioid dispensing. *Mitigant:* charges are being accrued as they crystallize and are small relative to ~$15B of LTM FFO; the risk is a judgment materially above accruals, which is a stated re-review trigger.
- **Care-delivery execution.** The $5,725M Health Care Delivery goodwill impairment, $288M of Accountable Care (ACO REACH) losses in FY2025 and clinic closures say the Oak Street / Signify thesis is unproven. *Mitigant:* the impairment is non-cash and the write-down reduces future earnings drag from this unit; group cash flow does not depend on it.
- **PBM and drug-pricing regulation.** Rebate reform, pricing transparency rules and GLP-1 cost inflation pressure Health Services and pharmacy margins; large PBM clients periodically re-bid. *Mitigant:* scale (87M plan members), specialty pharmacy growth, and the integrated model give CVS more offsets than a standalone PBM.
- **Refinancing cost.** Interest expense has risen from $2,287M (FY2022) to $3,119M (FY2025) as cheap pandemic-era coupons roll off. *Mitigant:* the ladder is long (68% of principal after 2030) and LTM coverage is 6.08x on the adjusted basis; no downgrade-acceleration clauses exist in the revolvers or notes.

## 8. Recommendation & conditions

**Approve** senior unsecured exposure to CVS Health Corporation at investment-grade terms, sized to a BBB internal assessment, with the following conditions:

1. **Exposure cap / re-review trigger:** total debt above $70B, or any single debt-funded acquisition above $5B, triggers a fresh committee review before further purchases.
2. **Monitoring tests (semi-annual, on filings):** adjusted gross leverage at or below 4.25x and FFO/debt at or above 15%, computed on the definitions in Section 4. Two consecutive breaches move the internal assessment to BBB− and freeze additions.
3. **Litigation watch:** any single judgment or settlement commitment above $1.5B beyond existing accruals triggers re-review.
4. **Tenor preference:** favor maturities inside the light 2027–2030 window of the ladder while the deleveraging program completes.

## 9. Methodology & verification

- **Sources.** SEC EDGAR XBRL company facts for CIK 0000064803, pulled October 20, 2026 (`pull_data.py` reproduces the pull; raw JSON in `data/`). Annual figures are the latest-filed 10-K values for each fiscal year; interim figures are from the Q2 2026 and Q2 2025 10-Qs. LTM = FY2025 − H1 2025 + H1 2026; balance-sheet items at period end.
- **Debt definition.** Long-term debt (noncurrent) + current portion + short-term borrowings, at carrying value. This ties exactly to the FY2025 10-K: total borrowings of $64,570M at December 31, 2025 per the borrowings note, and the maturity ladder sums to the $63,709M gross principal disclosed in the same note.
- **Adjustments.** Each add-back is a separate line item in the consolidated statements of operations or a discrete charge from the filings' segment reconciliations: FY2021 goodwill impairment $431M; FY2022 opioid litigation charges $5,803M and loss on assets held for sale $2,533M; FY2023 restructuring $507M and loss on assets held for sale $349M; FY2024 restructuring $1,179M and opioid accrual charge $100M; FY2025 goodwill impairment $5,725M, legacy litigation $1,220M (recorded H1 2025) and opioid accrual charge $320M (Q3 2025). Only the Q3 2025 items fall inside the LTM window.
- **FFO.** Approximation, labelled as such: net income + depreciation & amortization + goodwill impairment. It excludes deferred taxes, stock compensation and other non-cash items that a full agency FFO build would include.
- **Verification.** The companion workbook computes every ratio from the Inputs sheet with live formulas; after building, it was recalculated in LibreOffice and all 132 ratio cells matched an independent Python recomputation from `data/inputs.json` exactly (within rounding). Input values were additionally tied by hand to the FY2025 10-K balance sheet and borrowings note and to the Q2 2026 10-Q balance sheet (total debt $61,410M; cash $11,329M).
- **Ratings reference.** Agency ratings were verified during preparation from a public ratings database (InvestmentGrade.com) and are cited for reference only; the shadow rating is my own assessment.

## 10. Limitations

- Leverage is measured at period-end balances; intra-period peaks (e.g., commercial paper swings) are not captured. Commercial paper was $2,119M at December 31, 2024 and nil at both December 31, 2025 and June 30, 2026.
- Adjusted EBITDA depends on my judgment about which charges are non-recurring; reported-basis ratios are shown alongside so the committee can apply its own haircuts. Charges described as one-off have recurred in some form in three of the last five years.
- Insurance reserve adequacy (including premium deficiency reserves and the opioid accrual) is taken as reported; I have not independently audited reserves.
- H1 2026 figures are unaudited. June 2026 finance lease liabilities are carried at December 2025 values because the Q2 2026 condensed balance sheet does not break them out ($1,326M; immaterial to the conclusions).
- Figures are as most recently reported in comparative filings; immaterial reclassifications versus originally filed amounts (e.g., FY2022 operating income) are not adjusted.
- Agency ratings are cited from a secondary public database rather than agency releases; outlooks in particular may lag agency actions.
- This memo uses public information only. It is an independent educational project, not investment advice and not a credit rating.
